SCHEDULE A 
Research Plan 
 
Overview 
Year-2 studies identified two neuronal populations engaged during opioid use that differ in what they 
report. The program's primary focus is a subset of transcriptomically distinct populations in the 
basomedial amygdala (BMA) and orbitofrontal area (ORB) that are activated during volitional opioid 
seeking but not in yoked animals given the same passive dose — the dissociation that marks the 
addiction process rather than drug exposure. This differential is strongest at the post-test (~24 h) 
timepoint; the acute during-consumption window (~1 h) is dominated by drug exposure and cannot 
isolate the addiction-specific component. 
 
A second population — cortical intratelencephalic / cortico-thalamic (IT/CT) principal neurons of the 
anterior cingulate (ACA), infralimbic (ILA), prelimbic (PL), and orbitofrontal (ORB) areas, largely mu-
opioid-receptor-negative — undergoes opioid-induced transcriptional reprogramming (immediate-
early like Fos and Arc, clock genes like PER2, and pCREB-target genes) under both volitional and passive 
exposure, consistent with a shift in cortical excitation/inhibition (E/I) balance. Because these 
transcriptional changes persist well after opioids are cleared, when animals experience withdrawal and 
seek opioids, these changes are relevant to addiction. Independent studies in reward and motivated-
behavior research have shown that manipulating the activity of these cells alters addiction-related 
behaviors: for example, optogenetic manipulation of these cells produced a loss of control over reward 
consumption. However, a direct link with opioid addiction remains to be established.   Year 3 pursues 
three aims: (1) identify, at single-cell resolution by Xenium spatial transcriptomics, and cross-model 
validate (rat RNAscope/IHC of a small marker-gene panel) the activation of the BMA, ORB, and IT/CT 
ACA/IL/PL/ORB  populations; (2) test their causal contribution to opioid-seeking and addiction related 
behavior by chemogenetic manipulation; and (3) select and test an intervention on the identified 
population as proof-of-concept. The BMA, ORB, and IT/CT populations are carried in parallel through 
the Aim 2 causal test, and a single lead target is down-selected for Aim 3. 
 
1. Scientific Rationale 
The organizing principle is to determine, in causal experiments, the contribution to opioid seeking and 
addiction-related behaviors of the BMA, ORB and IT/CT cell populations. Opioid exposure per se — 
including passive and therapeutic administration — drives broad molecular and E/I changes, so no 
single marker is addiction-specific on its own. Addiction-specificity is defined operationally, by 
dissociation from matched non-contingent (yoked-passive): activation during volitional seeking but not 
under yoked-passive delivery of the same dose (strongest at the ~24 h post-test window), covariation of 
the signal with the degree of compulsive, escalated, or relapse-prone behavior across individuals, and 
persistence into protracted abstinence. Drug potency (morphine → heroin → fentanyl) and individual 
traits modulate vulnerability and are treated as sources of graded severity rather than nuisance.  
 
The previously proposed non-invasive EEG / E-I biomarker has been removed from scope: its resolution 
and the 9-month timeline do not support it, and existing human EEG/fMRI data already cover the 
translational gap. 
 
Three populations, tested in parallel. The basomedial amygdala (BMA) and orbitofrontal (ORB) 
populations carry the addiction-specific dissociation — activated during volitional but not yoked-
passive seeking — and define the program's addiction-versus-exposure claim. The anterior-cingulate 
IT/CT population, the most strongly recruited by opioids and the one whose genetic-access tools are 
already in hand, is carried alongside them as a causal / therapeutic candidate and pharmacology anchor, 
not as an addiction-specificity marker. All three are taken into the Aim 2 causal test, and a single lead 
target is down-selected on two criteria: the magnitude of the functional effect on volitional opioid

2. Validation Strategy 
The addiction-specific populations are identified by discovery transcriptomics and confirmed cross-
model, kept distinct by design: 
 
● Identification — spatial transcriptomics (Stanford) using the Xenium platform is applied to the 
volitional-versus-passive paradigm to resolve the BMA and ORB populations that dissociate 
active from passive animals at the ~24 h post-test window, yielding a small (3–5 gene) cell-type 
marker panel.  
● Cross-model validation —  the marker panel is validated in rat by RNAscope / antibody staining 
in cortex and amygdala (Hurd tissue; 1 h “during” and 24 h “post-test” timepoints), chosen over 
bulk RNA-seq for speed, and existing rat RNA-seq datasets (Hurd) serve as optional backup. 
 
Durability and severity —  a new rat fentanyl self-administration cohort tests whether the markers 
persist across abstinence (durability) and whether their magnitude scales with behavioral severity 
across individuals (matched-pharmacology addiction-specificity). 
 
3. Approach by Aim 
Aim 1 — Identify and cross-model validate the addiction-specific populations 
Xenium spatial transcriptomics resolves the BMA and ORB populations that dissociate volitional from 
passive seeking and yields a 3–5-gene marker panel (1a); the panel is validated by RNAscope/IHC in rat 
cortex and amygdala at the “during” (1 h) and “post-test” (24 h) windows (1b); a new rat fentanyl self-
administration cohort establishes durability across abstinence and correlation with behavioral severity 
(1c); all coordinated under an MTA with biweekly meetings and a marker-gene / pilot-tissue handoff 
between laboratories (1d). 
 
Aim 2 — Causal validation of the identified populations 
Following cell-type identification, chemogenetic manipulation (TRAP2 or cell-type-selective enhancer-
AAV with hM4Di / hM3Dq) of the identified cortical and amygdalar populations tests their necessity and 
sufficiency for volitional opioid seeking and addiction-like behavior (2a), with specificity controls 
establishing that effects are on the addiction-like phenotype rather than on locomotion and non-opioid 
reward, or therapeutic/passive responding (2b). Slice electrophysiology of the identified populations is 
the primary functional readout of the cellular phenotype. The BMA, ORB, and IT/CT populations are 
manipulated in parallel —  IT/CT with the enhancer-AAVs already validated in mouse and rat, BMA / 
ORB via TRAP2 or population-selective enhancer-AAV — and a single target is down-selected on causal 
effect on volitional seeking plus severity-scaling. Whether the BMA / ORB populations are themselves 
IT/CT-class principal cells sharing the same GPCRs (e.g., β1-adrenergic, 5-HT2A) is resolved at 
identification (Aim 1); if so, the three converge on one cell class and one toolset. 
 
Aim 3 — Intervention proof-of-concept 
A single lead target/intervention on the identified population is selected and tested in vivo, read out by 
(oral volitional-vs-passive, CPP, withdrawal, and side effects) (3a–3b). Aim 3 is gated on a positive 
causal result in Aim 2.

5. Milestones and Deliverables 
This project is staged so that spend is committed incrementally as evidence accrues. Activities marked 
“Now” in the table below proceed at signing; activities marked G1–G3 are released only when the 
corresponding gate is met. 
 
Gate 1 — Signature identified. The Aim 1a Xenium screen resolves a reproducible BMA / ORB 
population (or 3–5-gene marker panel) that separates volitional / addiction animals from yoked, 
passive-exposure animals at ~24 h post-test, reproducibly across ≥2 cohorts at a pre-registered 
threshold. Releases the Xenium-derived rat RNAscope panel (1b) and sequencing / analysis of the 
fentanyl cohort (1c). 
 
Gate 2 — Signature validated and targetable. The identified population reproduces across mouse 
Xenium, rat RNAscope, and the fentanyl cohort, and a working genetic-access handle exists — adequate 
TRAP2 capture efficiency, or an enhancer-AAV that labels the population specifically enough to 
manipulate. Releases the in-vivo chemogenetic manipulation (2a), its specificity controls (2b), and slice 
electrophysiology (2c). 
 
Gate 3 — Causal role confirmed. Chemogenetic manipulation of the identified population moves the 
addiction-relevant endpoint (volitional intake / severity / durability) directionally and specifically, not 
explained by locomotor or pharmacokinetic confounds. Releases Aim 3 target selection and the 
intervention proof-of-concept (3a–3b). 
 
Proceeds now, regardless of gates. Aim 1a Xenium identification (the entry point); the provisional-
panel RNAscope pilot on existing rat OFC heroin tissue — SATB2 identity, FOS, PER2 (1b); behavior and 
tissue collection for the fentanyl cohort (1c); and Aim 2 genetic-access preparation — TRAP2 colony / 
enhancer-AAV construction (2a) — begun in parallel so the causal experiment is not serialized behind 
colony build. OFC tissue is available now; rat amygdala tissue becomes available from the new Hurd 
cohorts.

6. Coordination and Timeline 
The laboratories operate under an MTA / in-kind tissue-sharing agreement with biweekly coordination. 
Stanford runs Xenium spatial transcriptomics and, with Scherrer, defines the 3–5-gene marker panel; 
the Hurd lab provides the tissue to validate the panel by RNAscope/IHC in rat cortex and amygdala and 
first supplies pilot tissue blocks for probe optimization. Because the Hurd tissue is limited and precious, 
the Scherrer lab runs a suitability pilot before committing the full set, and any unusable tissue and 
remainder are returned to the Hurd lab. A new rat fentanyl self-administration cohort provides 
durability and severity-correlation data. The EEG / non-invasive-biomarker effort and the circadian 
(ZT) cohort have been removed from scope. Circadian, sleep, and basic executive-function measures 
may still be collected opportunistically as exploratory, non-gating secondaries on existing cohorts if 
time allows, at no additional budget. Because incubation of craving is defined by an abstinence window 
(~30–45 days), the durability and downstream intervention work may extend into a subsequent phase.
